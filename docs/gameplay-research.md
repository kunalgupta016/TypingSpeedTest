# Typing game interaction research

Reviewed November 2, 2026. The game names in FingerFox are original; these references informed the interaction patterns, not copied art or text.

| Reference | Interaction pattern observed | FingerFox direction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Typing Rain](https://typingrain.com/) | Several falling words can be typed in any order. Enter clears a complete word. Missed words cost lives; clean clears build combo and score. | Word Rain now shows multiple moving targets, accepts any visible word, requires Enter to clear, and tracks score, combo, lives, and increasing fall speed. |
| [ZType how to play](https://ztype.com/how-to-play) | Words ride on descending ships. The first letter locks a target, typing the rest destroys it, mistakes hold the shot, and missed ships cost lives. | Star Blaster now has falling ship targets, first-letter lock, strict word completion, combo scoring, lives, and faster later waves. |
| [TypeLemon](https://www.typelemon.com/about/) | Separate solo racing, live progress bars, word-on-enemy shooter, and runner/ghost modes make typing affect a visible world state. | Race Day has a pace-car track; Lane Runner has an advancing lane marker; shooter modes have independent moving targets. |
| [TypeRacer](https://play.typeracer.com/?universe=classic) | Players race over the same quote, with progress tied to the text they finish. | Race Day keeps a shared text passage and visual competitor progress. Online opponents need a backend and remain unavailable. |
| [Monkeytype about](https://monkeytype.com/about) | A restrained test interface gives immediate per-character correctness, speed, and accuracy feedback, with configurable test modes. | Zen Test remains a quiet timed test; the other games use their own rules and feedback rather than only different descriptions. |

Research notes are based on public product and help pages read on November 2, 2026.
